6-month paid social retainer with a health brand

Concepts, hooks, scripts and on-camera content, built to be tested as paid ads on Meta/Instagram in a regulated category.

The challenge

Paid-social creative that felt like a real woman's story instead of an ad, in a category with strict rules on claims and framing. Every hook had to stop the scroll without crossing a compliance line. The goal: get people to click through and sign up.

The strategy

Every batch of videos was a test, not a one-off. Each round had a hypothesis, a single variable and a metric that decided the next round. Concepts and scripts stayed inside the brand's compliance rules before filming.

What I tested

Lead with the viewer's biggest objection, which is cost, then flip it into a benefit. Variable: the hook, with the body held steady. Judged by hook rate and CTR first, then CPA and ROAS.

The $133/month Reel, frame by frame

Hook
"Paying $133/month for my glp-1.."

Leads with the number people worry about, and the trailing dots open a loop.

Payoff
"but saving money on groceries, alcohol & takeaway.. so it all works out"

Turns the cost objection into net savings in one line.

Format
Selfie-style at home, iced coffee in hand

Reads like a friend's story, not an ad. It is framed around a personal budget, not medical outcomes.

Testing Roadmap

How I'd apply this to your brand

Every round is built so the result tells you what to do next.

1

Audit

Your ads and competitors' ads, to find the angles that are already saturated.

2

Roadmap

3 concepts, 2 hooks each, one variable per test.

3

Brief and script

Written inside your compliance rules, then filmed or directed.

4

Read the data

Hook rate, CTR, CPA and ROAS decide what to scale, iterate or kill.

5

Recap

A short recap after every batch, so the next round starts from data, not guesses.

Hypothesis

One clear belief about why an angle will win.

Variable

Only one thing changes, so the result is readable.

Metric

Hook rate and CTR for the opening, CPA and ROAS for the business.

Decision

Scale it, iterate it, or kill it.
